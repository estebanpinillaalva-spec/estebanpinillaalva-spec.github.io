Case 01 · Internship

The maintenance department still calculates its MTBF and MTTR with something I built, without me

With help from Claude, I automated in Excel the weekly calculation of the reliability indicators of an automotive maintenance department. They are still using it months after my internship ended.

15–20 min → ~2 minper weekly run (87–90% less)
1,370real maintenance records (March and April 2026)
7real data problems solved and documented
Company
Aludec S.A., manufacturer of decorative automotive components (CIE Automotive group)
Period
April and May 2026
Role
Intern in the Maintenance Department
Authorship
My own, built with help from Claude (AI). My internship supervisor authorised naming the company. Production data is not published.

The problem

Every week, the maintenance supervisor pulled the data from the internal database, calculated MTBF and MTTR by hand in a separate Excel sheet and then copied them into the tracking sheet. It took 15 to 20 minutes, it was always the same person, and a manual calculation like that is easy to get wrong.

What I did

  • A 17-sheet workbook where raw data enters through a single sheet, DATOS, and formulas distribute it to the analysis sheets by maintenance type and by week.
  • MTBF calculation (department hours ÷ number of corrective jobs) and MTTR (hours of the type × 60 ÷ number of corrective jobs), with hours per week, interventions, hours per type, monthly averages and yearly totals.
  • Nine maintenance types with fixed names: corrective and preventive maintenance for machines, moulds, peripherals and the plant, plus improvements.
  • A TUTORIAL sheet written for the department staff: the accepted names, which cells not to touch and what to check when something does not add up.

What went wrong and how I fixed it

The real data was not clean. These are the seven problems that came up:

#FailureCauseFix
1The week calculated across sheets returned 0WEEKNUM over ranges in another sheet was not evaluated in the environment where Claude tested the workbook (LibreOffice)Helper columns for month and week precalculated in the DATOS sheet itself
2March data did not land in the March columnsThe ISO week was used instead of the company's own week definitionWeek numbering from Sunday to Friday
32025 values in the first two weeksHard-coded cells inherited from the original templateCleanup of 27 hard-coded cells
4Weeks were counted as 7-day blocks of the monthFormula based on the day of the monthWeek of the year with a dynamic first Sunday, validated against a real week (128 records)
5Dates pasted as text were not counted (recurring)YEAR() does not work on textConversion to date and a text-tolerant formula
6A type written in the singular was not counted (recurring)The data used a different name from the one the formula looked forNormalisation and a validation drop-down with the 9 canonical names
7One column was left with fixed numbers when weeks were addedThe formula did not reach the new rowsFormula extended to row 5,000

I fixed the two recurring failures by restricting the input: a drop-down with the nine valid values and normalising the data before calculating. Tweaking the formula would not have been enough.

Result

  • From 15–20 minutes to about 2 minutes per run. On a weekly basis, that goes from 13–17 hours a year to 1.7 hours.
  • The calculation is deterministic and can be traced back to the source records.
  • The department still uses it without me. The time saving is modest. What I value most is that the tool kept working after I left, because I documented the handover.

Limitations

  • The formulas reach row 5,000 of DATOS; beyond that they need to be extended.
  • The workbook handles one current year and does not accumulate several in parallel.
